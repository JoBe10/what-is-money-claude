# Act III — The Provenance Map
## Every frame of Scenes 11–15, classified against the legacy deck

**Status: RULED, 2 September 2026.** The presenter approved the staging map and provenance sketch of `docs/act-3-kickoff.md` (§2–§3) in one pass — ruling 3 of the Act III states brief — and this document completes the sketch as the formal map, the way `docs/act-2-provenance.md` was completed. **It now governs all Act III work**, and every Act III session verifies a frame's class here before touching it (`AGENTS.md` §4.9).

**Classes:** **PORT** — a proven legacy treatment exists and is transplanted verbatim; redesign is forbidden and generating candidates is a process defect. **ADAPT** — a proven treatment with one ruled change, named here; only that change is made. **NEW** — no proven treatment exists; the full-coverage rule and the candidate process apply.

# 0. The convention this map classifies by

The Act II convention carries over unchanged, confirmed by the presenter on 31 August 2026 (`docs/act-2-provenance.md` §0):

> **A frame is classified by its treatment — not by its subject and not by its words.** Putting a different render or a different sentence through a proven treatment does not make the frame new. Changing *how the frame is made* does. Where a ruling changed the treatment itself, that ruling is the frame's ADAPT ruling.

# 2. The ladder's register boundary — the frame's first test

Carried forward from the 31 August stub, because it is the one clause a builder could get wrong:

**The register boundary is respected, and the ruling says so explicitly:** renders never enter a diagram (master §6.3). Lineup scale is the register's own sanctioned scale for a row of objects — it is what `ComparisonAssetHeader`'s `DISPLAY_BOX` and the carrier lineup of `4-06` already use, and what the rails law fixed film-wide as the shared band box (188 × 188 world, each render in a box of its own aspect, contain-fit; `AGENTS.md` §6) — and an object sequence is not a diagram. **The band form is the proof:** every render stands *above* the rising line at its stage's own clearance, bottom-aligned, no render ever standing on the drawn line; the dots, ticks, labels and lines beneath keep the ladder's drawn grammar unchanged. **This is the boundary the frame has to be built to clear, and the session that builds it treats clearing it as the frame's first test rather than its last.**

**The stage-mark assignments — presenter-ruled, 2 September 2026 (r2 ruling 3, master §13), superseding the r1 stand-ins.** The four stage marks are the presenter's own neutral renders, gated and ingested through the standard harness (`docs/dark-field-manifest.md` §2, the Act III neutral-marks drop): **collectible = `collectible`** (a raw gem crystal) · **store of value = `store`** (an hourglass) · **medium of exchange = `medium`** (a handshake) · **unit of account = `unit`** (a balance scale). ~~The r1 stand-ins — `single_cowrie` (ruled), `gold`, `coinage`, `ledger` (honest, flagged)~~ are retired from the ladder by this ruling, and the reason is the payoff it protects: **monetary assets appear on the ladder only as climbers.** Scene 14's coin lands at stage two as *the only monetary object in sight* — a ladder whose stage marks were themselves monetary goods would bury that landing. The same three job objects replace Scene 11's grammar glyphs at the triad's spokes, at the rails-law band scale, so the jobs and the stages are one visual family.

# 4. How this map governs

Every Act III session, before touching a frame:

1. **Look the frame up here.** A frame with no row is unclassified, and classifying it is the presenter's.
2. **PORT** — transplant the named treatment verbatim. Grade, type and stage wiring only. **A port that cannot reproduce its treatment without a design decision is a stop-and-flag, not a judgment call.**
3. **ADAPT** — make exactly the one named change. Everything else is a port.
4. **NEW** — the one NEW row is recorded as not built; nothing applies.

Amendments are presenter rulings only.
