# 0. The staging question, answered first — Act IV's anchor

Act II taught the pipeline to answer this before building anything: *what stays on screen and grows, so a viewer can follow by eye alone?* Act III's answer was the triad. Act IV's answer is already written into the frozen architecture, and this map records it rather than proposing it:

**The anchor is the Claim Mark itself — the through-line coming home.** Scene 16 opens on **the returned exchange frame from Scene 4 reconstructing** (the architecture: *"The saved frame from Scene 4 reconstructs; everything recedes but the claim."*) — the film's own approved Act I frame (`review/gate-2/states` — `s4-b4-b`, the claim resting on the save road with the road drawing on into black, and `s4-b5-b`, the closing pair beneath it), rebuilt by the Act I stage, then receding until only the claim stands. That is the homecoming: the disc the viewer has watched since Scene 3 is the thing the act is about to test. From that moment the register shifts to Act IV's abstraction — black, white, and the accent at full deployment (master §8.5) — and **the proven Section 4 treatments are their own world thereafter**: the claim inside its carrier shell (the `ClaimObject`-in-`CarrierShell` scene, construction C) is the act's persistent object through Scenes 16–19 — the legacy's own continuity, carried across five slides as *"the carrier, inherited"* (4-06 → 4-07 → 4-08 → 4-09 → 4-10): it acquires a body, crosses time, sets out into the century, meets the unknowns, and goes on the bench; the two failure indexes (Scenes 20–21) and the inversion grid with the ten properties (Scene 22) are the typographic world the bench opens onto; the comparison (Scene 23) is the structural world the act ends in.

**Why the anchor is sound without a candidate.** Prototype Gate 1 ruled the Claim Mark **candidate A — the current ClaimObject luminous disc, verbatim** (`docs/batch-a-selections-report.md`; `src/proto/claim-mark.js`, `CLAIM_MARK_SELECTION = 'A'`). The legacy `ClaimObject` renders the shared `LuminousDisc` (its own header: *"literally 1.2's disc"*); Act III's token at the triad's center is the same `luminous-disc` by its own classes; Act I's held claim is the same disc in the exchange stage. So the object that reconstructs at Scene 16, the object at the triad's center one beat earlier, and the object inside the carrier shell for the rest of the act are **one drawn disc**, film-wide — there is no handoff to design, only a morph to wire.

**The entry seam, after the Act III final rulings (3 September 2026, master §13).** The act is now entered from the pivot coda — Scene 15 ends on the returned triad with the disc at its center, STORE OF VALUE at full voice, and the store-of-value question in the statement slot (`s15-b7`); the tower's held question stands one beat earlier and is still the question Scene 16 answers. The homecoming therefore begins with the disc already on stage: the triad's center *is* the claim, and the Scene 4 frame can reconstruct around it. **That is Session 2's to render and the presenter's to see** — this map records the seam's two ends (the approved `s15-b7` and the approved `s4-b4-b` / `s4-b5-b`) as film material already proven, and leaves its motion to the states sheet as one honest render with a plain-English flag (§4, seam 1).

**What the map does not decide.** How much of the Scene 4 frame reconstructs (the whole fork at rest, or the save road with the held claim alone — the architecture says *"the saved frame"*, which is `s4-b4-b`); the moment of the register shift; the disc's scale at the homecoming against its Section 4 scale inside the shell (the legacy ClaimObject's default 116, and 176 released at 4-04 b4). These are wiring, flagged on the cells; none is a design decision.
