# 3. Intellectual foundation

## 3.1 Value

Value is subjective. It is not a physical substance contained in labour hours, objects, or money.

The surgeon example must not imply that an hour of time has a universal value. The market values a particular service in a particular context. The service may combine scarce knowledge, training, judgment, dexterity, responsibility, institutional resources, and demand.

Do not imply the labour theory of value.

## 3.2 The unfinished exchange

When someone provides a specific good, service, asset, or form of value to the market and receives money, the ultimate exchange has not yet been completed.

The person has given something specific but has not yet selected the final goods or services they want in return.

The conceptual sequence is:

> **specific contribution → monetary counterclaim → future counter-value**

## 3.3 Definition of money

The preferred formal wording is:

> **Money is the transferable counterclaim created when value is provided to the market without the final counter-value yet being taken.**

Accessible supporting language:

> **Money carries the unfinished half of an exchange.**

> **A legitimate monetary claim must originate somewhere in value provided. Once created, rightful ownership of that claim can be transferred, gifted, inherited, or exchanged.**

The word **claim** is economic, not a legal demand against a named debtor. Where clarity is needed, use:

> **a transferable purchasing-power claim on the market**

The framework is inspired by Alfred Lansburgh, writing as Argentarius. A restrained source acknowledgment may appear in speaker notes, end credits, or the public video description. It does not need to interrupt the visible narrative.

## 3.4 Claim versus carrier

The economic function and the asset carrying it are distinct.

> **The claim is the economic function. The asset is the carrier.**

Historical carriers may include shells, precious metals, paper notes, bank ledgers, and Bitcoin.

The battery metaphor may be used only after the claim/carrier distinction is established:

> **If the claim is the charge, the monetary asset is the battery carrying it.**

Do not use “economic energy” as the formal definition of money. It may remain a secondary metaphor, not the conceptual foundation.

## 3.5 Saving

> **Spending redeems the claim now. Saving carries the claim forward.**

Saving is the decision to keep the final exchange open.

Preferred definition:

> **A store of value is a monetary carrier that preserves the integrity and usability of an unredeemed claim through time.**

- **Integrity:** the claim is not arbitrarily diluted, altered, forged, seized, or subordinated by discretionary rules.
- **Usability:** the future holder can access, verify, divide, transfer, and exchange it.

## 3.6 Relative claim and dilution

The simplified model may show a holder owning a relative share of outstanding monetary claims.

Use this wording:

> **At the moment a person is paid, their money represents a particular relative claim on the goods and services being offered through the market.**

> **A sound monetary carrier prevents that relative claim from being arbitrarily diluted from the money side.**

The model may compare:
- output rising while the claim pool stays fixed;
- claims rising while output does not.

Non-negotiable lines:

> **Currency units can be created. Purchasing power cannot be printed.**

> **You can print claims. You cannot print what the claims are trying to buy.**

> **Without corresponding production, new claims do not enlarge the pool of real goods and services. They alter who can command it.**

This is an intentionally simplified illustration, not a literal assertion that a monetary unit grants legal ownership of a fixed percentage of every asset or all subjective “economic value.” Speaker notes must acknowledge that actual purchasing power is also affected by preferences, demand to hold money, credit, productivity, population, market structure, and the goods offered for exchange.

## 3.7 Market stability versus monetary-architecture stability

Two different questions are often hidden inside the word **stability**.

### Stability of market valuation
- demand;
- liquidity;
- leverage;
- expectations;
- marginal bids and offers;
- price.

### Stability of monetary architecture
- supply rules;
- ownership rules;
- verification;
- settlement;
- control.

Core lines:

> **Architecture protects the integrity of the claim. The market determines what the claim can buy.**

> **A freely formed market price is the best available coordination signal for changing preferences and scarcity conditions—not an omniscient measure of fundamental value.**

> **A superficially smooth price cannot compensate for an architecture that systematically dilutes the claim.**

> **But sound architecture alone cannot guarantee purchasing power. A perfectly scarce asset that nobody wants will not preserve value.**

> **Bitcoin does not fix its price. It fixes the rules through which the market discovers its price.**

> **The feature is not volatility. The feature is that adjustment happens through price rather than through discretionary changes to supply.**

> **Price should be free to move. The monetary rules should be hard to move.**

Bitcoin’s current high volatility should be described as a cost or symptom of monetising a new fixed-supply asset, not as the objective and not as an inherent virtue. Greater adoption, liquidity, and market depth may reduce volatility, but this is an expectation rather than a guarantee.

## 3.8 Saving, investing, and native yield

Use **native yield**, not merely cash flow.

A self-custodied bitcoin does not generate additional bitcoin merely by existing. Yield can be created by lending bitcoin, selling options, providing liquidity, or using structured products, but that yield comes from an added arrangement rather than from Bitcoin itself.

Core distinction:

> **Saving preserves a general monetary claim. Investing deploys that claim into a specific economic arrangement in pursuit of a return.**

Core yield line:

> **Yield is compensation for providing capital, liquidity, time, balance-sheet capacity, or insurance—or for accepting risk, dependence, or dilution.**

Core saving line:

> **Saving should not require taking additional enterprise, credit, or counterparty risk merely to preserve purchasing power.**

Bitcoin is not risk-free. The advantage is not the absence of yield by itself.

> **The absence of native yield is not the advantage. The absence of a required yield-producing counterparty or enterprise is.**

> **Bitcoin’s monetary thesis does not depend on a tenant, borrower, management team, issuer, or productive enterprise successfully generating that yield.**

Final bridge:

> **Under depreciating money, investing has become the new saving.**

## 3.9 Civilization

The section should reveal, not fully prove, the broader thesis.

Money lets unfinished exchanges cross people, places, and generations. It coordinates specialised strangers who may never meet.

Core lines:

> **Money lets unfinished exchanges cross people, places, and generations.**

> **Money is civilization’s coordination protocol.**

> **Money does not determine civilization. It changes the payoff to patience.**

> **It does not dictate every decision. It changes the incentive gradient beneath every decision.**

Do not expand this section into detailed arguments about war finance, family formation, Cantillon effects, cultural decline, or every consequence of time preference. Those belong in the future dedicated presentation.

# 6. Recurring visual language

## 6.1 The Open Claim Circuit

The recurring monetary-claim motif is an **open claim circuit**, first introduced and fully explained on slide 4.04. It must not appear unexplained on the section opener.

Its canonical labelled form is:

```text
VALUE PROVIDED  ●────────○  FINAL COUNTER-VALUE
  COMPLETE                       PENDING
```

Meaning:
- filled left node: something valuable has already been provided;
- hollow right node: the final goods or services ultimately wanted have not yet been received;
- orange connecting path: the earned, transferable monetary claim keeping the exchange open;
- filled right node later: the claim has been redeemed and the exchange has closed;
- outer shell later: the monetary carrier embodying and transporting the abstract claim.

The circuit should be:
- unmistakably geometric and restrained;
- Bitcoin orange without excessive glow;
- visually premium and cinematic;
- recognisable across the full section;
- capable of transforming for spending, saving, embodiment, dilution, obstruction, control, degradation, verification, fungibility, historical testing, and eventual redemption;
- clearly distinct from a coin, dollar sign, battery, magical energy orb, glowing gem, or loading indicator.

Meaning across the planned section:
1. it emerges from the surgeon’s unfinished exchange on 4.04;
2. spending fills the pending node and closes the exchange;
3. saving leaves the node open and carries the circuit forward;
4. a carrier shell forms around it;
5. its relative position is preserved or diluted in the simplified model;
6. it travels toward 2126;
7. failure modes threaten the carrier and claim;
8. the ten properties appear as protections against those failures;
9. candidate carriers and their rulebooks are compared;
10. the claim reaches a future market;
11. individual circuits multiply into a civilization-wide coordination network.

Every transformation must have conceptual meaning. The circuit is never decorative.

### 4.04 — The Unfinished Exchange

#### Purpose
Answer what the surgeon received and establish the central definition and visual grammar:
- he has not received the final goods or services he actually wants;
- he received an earned, transferable claim on value;
- standardised, widely accepted money untethers the claim from the patient;
- he can take it back to the broader market and exchange it with willing sellers;
- the final counter-value remains pending;
- the exchange is still open.

#### Spoken script
> He has not received the final goods or services he actually wants.
>
> If he had received sneakers, food, wine or some other final good directly, that would have been barter. Instead, he receives money.
>
> What does that money represent at its core?
>
> It represents an earned, transferable claim on value. The surgeon has provided a service that was exchanged for four hundred dollars, but he has not yet taken the final four hundred dollars of goods or services he wants in return.
>
> Because money is standardised and widely accepted, that claim is no longer tied to the patient. The surgeon can take it back to the broader market and exchange it with anyone willing to accept the money.
>
> Money therefore turns a person-specific exchange into a transferable claim on the wider market.
>
> Or more formally: money is the transferable counterclaim created when value is provided to the market without the final counter-value yet being taken.
>
> The surgeon has completed his side of the exchange. The final counter-value remains pending. The exchange is still open.

Transition:
> And once we see money this way, saving becomes much clearer: spending closes the exchange, while saving keeps it open.
