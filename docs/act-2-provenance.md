# Act II — The Provenance Map
## Every frame of Scenes 5–10, classified against the legacy deck

**Status: A PROPOSAL. Nothing here is decided.** This document exists to be ruled on in one pass. Until the presenter rules it, no row governs anything, and the session that produced it decided no classification — that is the brief's instruction and `AGENTS.md` §4.9's own rule: *classifying a frame is the presenter's, through his act's provenance map.*
**Scope:** the fourteen style frames of `docs/batch-b-package.md` §3, ◆ and non-◆ alike.
**Classes:** **PORT** — a proven legacy treatment exists and is transplanted verbatim. **ADAPT** — a proven treatment exists with one ruled change, and the ruling is named. **NEW** — no proven treatment exists; the full-coverage rule and the candidate process apply.
**Date:** 30 August 2026

# 0. The convention this map classifies by

Every row below applies one rule, stated here so it can be rejected as a whole rather than argued row by row:

> **A frame is classified by its treatment — not by its subject and not by its words.** Putting a different render or a different sentence through a proven treatment does not make the frame new. Changing *how the frame is made* does. Where the architecture ruled a change to the treatment itself, that ruling is the frame's ADAPT ruling.

The consequence is deliberate: Scene 7's vault study is a PORT even though no vault has ever been on screen, because the display-scale study treatment is approved and shipped, and a subject is not a treatment. If the presenter reads provenance more narrowly — that a frame is only a PORT when the *same picture* already exists — then S6-F1, S7-F1, S8-F1 and S9-F2 move to NEW together, and he should say so once here rather than four times later.

# 2. ARGUABLE — one line per side, no recommendation

These are the only four rows that need the presenter to think rather than to confirm.

## S5-F1 ◆ — the through-line composition

> *the held claim with a carrier formed around it, the first carrier as `shells`*

- **PORT.** The composition exists and was already judged: `4-06-claim-and-carrier` puts the claim inside `CarrierShell`, whose construction was drawn three ways at shipping scale and selected (C, the banded wall, R7.2) — and Batch B's own spec asks for *"the carrier-context ClaimMark, centered per the presenter's symmetry ruling"*, which names that object.

## S5-F3 ◆ — the Zanzibar receipt

> *the evidence treatment; this treatment returns for every dated fact in the film*

- **PORT.** The deck already has a dated-fact treatment and it is strong: `2-07-the-severance` lands **`1971.`** alone at display scale and follows it with one decree sentence — and the Zanzibar receipt itself already exists, as the `receipt` wound on the shells stop in `2-04-the-competition-record` build 7.
- **NEW.** Both proven forms are *attached* — one to a rail stop, one to a rail camera frame — and Batch B asks for a standalone system that must carry `ZANZIBAR · 1800s · fact` and `1971 · fact` in the same grammar; a treatment that has only ever run once, welded to a component the architecture retired, has not been proven as a system.

## S7-F2 — the detachment

> *the certificate leaving the vault's orbit, the dependency line back*

- **ADAPT.** The deck has drawn this once already: in `2-07-the-severance` the paper rung leaves the ladder and settles as a marker floating above the line — the claim detaching from its base, with the ruled change being that Scene 7 keeps the dependency line the severance cuts.
- **NEW.** The severance's entire meaning is that the tether **breaks** (`R2.1 §D.2`: *every money in the record sits on the line; the current one hovers over it*), and Scene 7's is that it **holds**; a drawing whose argument is a surviving dependency is not the same drawing with one parameter changed — and Scene 7's two objects are dark-field renders where the severance was pure line.

## S8-F2 ◆ — the four-currency chart at film grade

> *the frozen data restyled to the deck's system*

- **PORT.** `2-07-the-severance` already renders this chart and every judgment in it is made: one vertex per observed year (*"the line is the record, not a curve fit"*), per-series alpha carrying the emphasis, the 1971 = 100 reference line, the end-label spread when USD and GBP finish within a label's height, the index note. It shipped. Nothing in the architecture rules a change to it.
- **ADAPT.** Batch B says *at film grade*, and Scene 8's chart stands alone where the legacy's stood over a dimmed rail inside a section-shaped slide — so the frame around the plot changes even though no datum does, and naming that change is what an ADAPT ruling is for.

# 4. ADAPT — the proven treatments with one ruled change

## S6-F2 ◆ — the elimination funnel

- **Legacy source:** `src/slides/section-2-origin/05-two-survivors.js` and `src/components/section-2/ElementGrid.js` — the stylized periodic table rising over the dimmed rail, then five elimination waves, each one an auto-timed gesture on its own advance, each with its own line beneath: *Anything that floats away is out · Anything that rusts, burns, or dissolves is out · Anything that kills the holder is out · Anything that will not hold a shape is out* — then the furnace wave, and the verdict *Workable nobility leaves two.*
- **The ruling that defines the one change:** synthesis architecture, **Ruling 3** — *"The periodic elimination: **RULED: kept, compressed** to one continuous beat inside Scene 6."*
- **The one change:** five separately-advanced waves become one continuous beat. Nothing else — not the population, not the eliminated regions, not the wave language, not the verdict.
- **Note:** the furnace wave is not decoration and must survive the compression. It exists by ruling R-03 (R4.1) because chemistry alone leaves the noble family, not two survivors — and Scene 10 needs palladium to be a real candidate that lost on **timing**, not one chemistry had already excluded. Compressing the sequence must not compress that distinction out.

## S10-F2 — palladium: the bar

- **Legacy source:** `src/slides/section-3-function/05-the-palladium-test.js` — the hook, the two-panel chart (`ANNUAL MINE SUPPLY · TONNES` as relative bars with the figures stated, beside `PRICE OF ONE OUNCE · MODERN ERA` on a log axis, one vertex per observed year), the 1803 timing line, the narrowed-role line, and the bar sentence. The data is real and sourced (`src/data/palladium.js`; SOURCES WIM-PD-001/002).
- **The ruling that defines the one change:** synthesis architecture, **Ruling 4** — *"Palladium: **RULED: placed in Scene 10**, as the bar the trade-off strip must clear."*
- **The one change:** the beat moves out of Act III's judging position and stands against the strip, compressed to the architecture's Scene 10 beats 3–4.
- **Note:** the two-epoch honesty (R3.1 §C) is load-bearing and travels with the port. The price panel invites a fair objection — gold was not the world's money by 1990 — and the beat answers it by running palladium against **both** of gold's eras. Compressing it into one epoch would reintroduce the confusion the legacy slide was rewritten to remove.

# 7. How to rule this map

The map is designed for one pass. For each of the fourteen rows, one of three answers settles it:

1. **Confirm** the proposed class.
2. **Change** it — name the class, and for ADAPT name the ruling that defines the one change.
3. For the four ARGUABLE rows, **choose a side** — or reject the framing and say what the row actually is.

Two answers settle more than one row each, and are worth giving first:

- **The convention in §0.** Confirm it, or narrow it. Narrowing moves S6-F1, S7-F1, S8-F1 and S9-F2 from PORT to NEW in one word.
- **S8-F2 and S5-F3** decide whether two of the systems sheet's five selections are still live.

Once ruled, this document governs all Act II work, and every Act II session verifies a frame's class here before touching it.
